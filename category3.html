East of Moldy Carrots and Rusted Buckets

01

Synopsis

A spiteful boy struggles to find his footing in a world of orcs and ogres.

Excerpt

In the grassy plains coasting the shore to the east of the Mediterranean sat a plump hill. It was peculiarly positioned, as close to the grainy sand as the beach would allow, while rising quite steeply and suddenly to a height of nearly twenty feet tall. In fact, this unusual protrusion from the meadow had grown so discreetly that it had caught the neighboring municipality unawares, forcing the townspeople to relocate houses, bakeries, infirmaries, churches and the town hall west of Lake Assad. Unfortunately, they forgot the farm and Henry. Henry was quite bitter he'd been left behind. Perhaps it was the result of his father forcing the neon-red barn in his unwilling inheritance or maybe he wasn't too fond of pork, but the thirty or so pigs he was left with were now drooling and squealing all over the morning dew. "What disgusting creatures," Henry muttered as he lifted himself out of bed, "I hear bacon's in season this time of year." With heavy steps, he trudged through the open barn door, grabbed a rusting pail of pre-cut and prepped rotting carrots and made his way outside. Henry winced, the pigs were uglier in person. And Henry didn't like ugly things. He quickly strode into the enclosure, locked the door and faced the swine. The pigs snorted in response. They were indeed quite varying, Henry thought. There were obese ones, fat ones, drooling ones, saggy ones, grisly ones, all putrid and all staring lustfully at his bucket. "How sinful," Henry sneered. But there was a smaller pig, shying away from the group surrounding him that appeared to be shunned in the corner. Henry grinned. Putting down his bucket, he took soft steps and slowly found himself face to face with the pig. Henry grinned again. And began viciously slamming his foot against the underside of the baby's belly.

Daniel Washington

02

Synopsis

A slice of life between a bitter mother and vengeful son when their dog gets in the way.

Excerpt

Daniel Washington was Margaret's only companion after her husband took his leave, and he smelled rich. Not rich like nickel coins or cotton bills, but rich like a squash soup; left too long in the oven, reduced to too thick a paste. He smelled earthy from the flesh of the fruit and the roasted skins brought forth an aroma of nuttiness, the orange kind. Margaret smiled and looked down, still remembering when the poodle had first come home. Daniel Washington had trotted in on his three remaining legs, sat on the carpet and pissed himself dry. He'd barked at Margaret's bloated pregnant stomach until his growls came shallow and hoarse and he'd slept, drenched in urine and the whispering fragrance of pine needles and twigs. The baby had stopped kicking then, perhaps fearing the small pup and Margaret had chuckled. A fearful child would be an easy child. The baby had taken the entire night to push, its shoulders had gotten stuck forcing Margaret to scream in agony for as long as it took for her voice to die. Oh, how she wished she was holding a scalpel and could end the screaming right then. And the baby had become a toddler, waddling around in its ugly legs like it was the king of its own dream world. The toddler left for school and Margaret breathed a sigh of relief, a whole day she could be with Daniel Washington alone together. And the boy never even cared for her child, her poodle. He dismissed the dog from his life and only faced Daniel Washington to throw withering glares and upturned eyes. Boys were always jealous, Margaret thought, but children would forever be despicable.

Cedar and Oak

03

Synopsis

As an elderly tree, Hoboken has long since seen the wonders of the world. But what happens when his patience is tested by young tiny shrubs.

Excerpt

"In the beginning, before man, before birds, before wind, and before that stingy old oak past the clearing, there lived dragons. Dragons, you may not know, are the ancestors of wings. They fly, they cry, and they are quite fond of their nests. You shall never see a mother dragon leave her eggs, and you shall never hear of a father dragon leaving his hoard. They- what is that awful piercing?" A slippery whistle had entered the gathering and all the trunks reverberated along with it. "It must be a dragon!" a voice screamed. "It's come to fly and protect its eggs!" another shrieked. "Calm, children!" Professor Hoboken's voice rang. "It'll be the witches then." The children stared wondrously up at the great cedar in awe. Witches, what a wonderful word that rolled across their tongue. The sound had disappeared and Professor Hoboken began again. "In the beginning, before mothers, before sky, before dreams, and before that damned nasty oak past the clearing, there lived witches. Potion brewers, these witches were and rightfully so they were called. They made concoctions of evil desire and young ones, I beg you listen close. The vials containing these mixtures enchant, intoxicate, love, and kill. They are not to be trifled with."

"An Exuberance of Flatulence"

04

Synopsis

An unusual encounter with a bank teller, a totally lost woman, and a man with a mission.

Excerpt

The arching ceilings reflected the dim glow of the evening sun, casting long shadows across the pseudo-marble floor. Pillars carved from plastic rising towards the top of the bank were spotlighted, like a deer caught in headlights. Carlos sat, facing the opposite wall and as the heat of the sun began creeping up his leg, he rose, strode from the bench and made his way to the only open kiosk. "I'd like to open an account." The bank manager eyed Carlos, his gaze shifting to the bulge in his shirt. "I'm sorry, but I'm currently helping another customer, you'll have to wait." He gestured towards a woman who had just entered the bank, drenched in sunlight and the blasting wind.

A Dose of Raspberry Jam

05

Synopsis

Two brothers investigate the suspicious, almost too suspicious death of the neighborhood baker.

Excerpt

It was twenty to two on a Sunday afternoon when the baker died. Huvven was suffering the driest winter it had seen in a decade. The roads were clear of frost and the missing blanket of white had left the inhabitants in a hypnotic daze. It was strange, for the city to be completely void of snow during this time, and almost as means of compensation, the winter wind had grown to be the harshest, most brittle cold yet. But despite the disgruntled skiers' complaints, threats from now unemployed snow plowers, and an increase of head injuries from homemade snowballs, the police station had yet to have seen the regular bustle of activities. Reported burglaries were at an all time low from the excitement of Christmas week and street crooks were too busy snooping around shops, looking for the best purchase that they could consider a steal. Even the multiple break-ins on Christmas Eve were found to be parents or neighborhood friends sneaking presents under the trees–the three kids watching Santa undress into their father were rightfully horrified. So when the phone rang at twelve fifteen on the day after Christmas, the policemen at the station thought nothing of it. That is until they heard grunting sounds and the thud of a body on the other line.
